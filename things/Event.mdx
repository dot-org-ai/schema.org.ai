# Event

An Event is a business occurrence: the immutable, past-tense record that something happened — a receiving event, a payment captured, a document signed — carrying the What/Who/When/Where/Why/How dimensions of the occurrence. In the verb-conjugation frame, an Event is the **"created"** form: the fact, after the act.

## The bucket ruling (org.ai ADR 0004, first application)

**Event is NATIVE, SHADOWING [`schema.org/Event`](https://schema.org/Event), and SEAM-MAPPED to GS1 EPCIS 2.0.** Running the admission decision tree:

- **Q1 (borrow) — no.** Merging our occurrence records into schema.org's Event (a concert, lecture, or festival listing) is meaning-destroying, not truth-preserving.
- **Q2 (extension) — no; the lineage veto fires.** An EPCIS-shaped receiving event is not truthfully a `schema:Event` (a scheduled public happening with attendees). Event therefore declares **no** `subClassOf schema:Event` edge.
- **Q3 (false friend) — yes: shadow.** Same name, different referent. The bare context term `Event` binds to `https://schema.org.ai/Event`; `https://schema.org/Event` remains one full IRI away for anyone marking up a concert. Register proximity breaks the cross-canon tie: the builder of an autonomous business who types `Event` means "something that happened in the business," not "a concert listing."
- **+R17 seam.** GS1 EPCIS 2.0's sense *aligns* — an Event is a past-tense verb, a business occurrence with What/Who/When/Where/Why/How dimensions — so EPCIS integrates as a protocol seam: context composition, CBV bizSteps as verb vocabulary, identifiers by reference. Never by adopting its meta-model or keys as native.

## Shadowing, concretely

| You write | It means |
|---|---|
| `"$type": "Event"` | `https://schema.org.ai/Event` — the business occurrence |
| `"$type": "https://schema.org/Event"` | schema.org's Event — the scheduled public happening |
| `"$type": "schema:Event"` | same as the full schema.org IRI (prefix declared in the context) |

External senses never lose their IRIs; at most they lose the bare term.
