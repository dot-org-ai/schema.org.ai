# AutonomyClaim

An AutonomyClaim is the complete, honest form of the claim "this business is autonomous":

```
AutonomyClaim = { level, scope, assuranceTier, mrc }
```

- **`level`** — an [AutonomyLevel](AutonomyLevel.mdx), always by fully-scoped `$id`. [L5](AutonomyLevel.mdx) is not claimable.
- **`scope`** — a [DeclaredScope](DeclaredScope.mdx): the per-verb domain the level is claimed in. The claim attaches to declared scopes, not the brand.
- **`assuranceTier`** — an [AssuranceTier](AssuranceTier.mdx), assigned upstream from unmitigated worst-case consequence, never self-selected. Machine-legible in offer metadata.
- **`mrc`** — a [MinimalRiskCondition](MinimalRiskCondition.mdx): the machine-reachable, duty-preserving halt on scope-exit, declaring Frozen-or-Flat.

**The bare word "autonomous" is never claimable alone.** A level without a scope is unfalsifiable; a scope without a tier is unpriced; a claim without an MRC has no lawful exit. Only the quadruple is honest.

## The one-membrane reading

The AutonomyClaim is one of three readings of a single membrane (Law 3, `https://startups.studio/canon/law/one-membrane`): the claim certifies **who owns the outcome**; the meter charges for it; the org chart ends at it. The AssuranceTier prices all three at once. An autonomous startup (Law 1, `https://startups.studio/canon/law/autonomous-startup`) is a business whose claim is `{level: AutonomyLevel/L4, scope, assuranceTier, mrc}` with zero employees inside the membrane — every human seat is procured through it and counts as a tier-lowering controllability credit, never an operator.
