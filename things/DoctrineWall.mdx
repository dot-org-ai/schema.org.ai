# DoctrineWall

A DoctrineWall is a **constitutive constraint**: structurally verifiable, reopenable only by governance. Per the R12-2 constitutive/mitigating cut, walls **DEFINE the hazard class** a business is tiered in; **mitigating controls are excluded from tier assignment** and become the tier's demanded evidence instead.

## The constitutive/mitigating cut

- **Constitutive** (a wall): the constraint is part of what the business *is*. Structurally verifiable — its absence would be visible in the architecture, not just in policy. Example: deletion structurally unavailable on an [EvidenceRegister](EvidenceRegister.mdx) (object-lock); no-custody-ever on a trading product; no publish path for unfiled matter.
- **Mitigating** (not a wall): a control layered on a capability that still exists. Example: table-format immutability where deletes are supported verbs you promise not to use; a two-step confirm dialog (friction, not authority).

**Anti-gaming test:** *remove the constraint — is the residue the same business?* If yes, it was mitigation. If no, it was constitutive. Classification is performed upstream, never by the builder.

## Consequences

- **Wall removal is a re-tiering event** (the licensure-event-rule interlock): a business that drops a wall is a different hazard class and must be re-assigned its [AssuranceTier](AssuranceTier.mdx).
- **The wall list and the [DeclaredScope](DeclaredScope.mdx) exclusions are one object read twice** — scope and tier are not independent coordinates.
- A wall in seam position is a [NeverMintWall](SeamClass.mdx): a verb the estate never mints.
